tRNA: a cloverleaf in 2D, an L shape in 3D

The main view uses tRNA coordinates from ribosome complexes. The acceptor, D, anticodon and TΨC arms fold through long-range contacts; the D and TΨC loops meet at the elbow. The cloverleaf diagram represents secondary structure, not the spatial shape.

What does RNA do during translation?

The tRNA anticodon recognizes the mRNA codon. 16S rRNA checks pairing geometry. The peptidyl transferase center (PTC) is formed by 23S rRNA, which positions substrates and supports catalysis. Proteins and translation factors remain essential for assembly, stability, selection and translocation.

The motion interpolates structural endpoints and is not a molecular dynamics simulation. Hybrid states, translocation and head swivel are schematic; not every intermediate coordinate has experimental support. Extended mRNA and nascent peptide paths are illustrative. The new peptide is shown unfolded. EF-G is described during translocation but its structure is not shown. The PTC highlight marks a functional region, not a separate molecule.
